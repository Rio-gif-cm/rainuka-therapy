# CRITIC #3: RUTHLESS COPY/TONE/VOICE CRITIQUE
## Rainuka Therapy Website

**Prepared:** January 2026  
**Scope:** Homepage, About, Booking, Pricing, Contact, FAQ, ADHD page  
**Focus:** Authenticity, warmth, headline power, microcopy clarity, consistency, clichés, specificity  
**Total Issues Found:** 28 critical copy problems  

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The site sounds like it's trying to be warm but keeps defaulting to therapy-speak and corporate softening. Rainuka's actual voice—vulnerable, specific, pattern-breaking—shines in the About page but gets diluted everywhere else. Headlines are safe when they should be arresting. Microcopy is helpful but generic. The copy jumps between intimate confessional tone and clinical positioning, leaving it unclear who Rainuka actually is to a stranger.

**The fatal flaw:** The copy describes vulnerability without *being* vulnerable. It talks about authenticity while using hedging language that undermines authenticity.

## 28 COPY PROBLEMS (QUOTED, ANALYZED, FIXED)

### **PROBLEM #1: Bland, Defensive Hero Headline**

**Location:** Homepage Hero  
**Text:** "Therapy that fits the life you actually have"

**Why it fails:**  
This is the single most important sentence on the site. It promises fit and practicality but sounds like every therapist's tagline. "Fits the life you actually have" is vague—*of course* therapy should fit your life. This doesn't differentiate Rainuka. It's defensive positioning ("I'm not one of those impractical therapists") rather than bold positioning.

**The problem beneath:** The headline is trying to be reassuring instead of compelling. It's answering an objection ("your therapist will be unrealistic") instead of creating desire.

**Better version:**  
"Therapy for the gap between who you look like and who you are"  
(This is specific to Rainuka's positioning—the outside/inside split she talks about constantly. It's magnetic.)

### **PROBLEM #2: "Trauma-Informed" Is Jargon Disguised as Honesty**

**Location:** Homepage Approach section  
**Text:** "I track your nervous system. Healing happens at your pace, not mine."

**Why it fails:**  
"Trauma-informed" is therapy industry jargon. Everyone says this. The explanation ("I track your nervous system") is slightly better, but it's still clinical language, not lived language. If Rainuka has actually experienced trauma, the language should feel like it comes from *inside* that experience, not from a textbook.

**The unfixed part:** The second sentence is good ("Healing happens at your pace, not mine"), but it comes after jargon, so it feels like corporate-speak trying to sound human.

**Better version:**  
"I don't push. Your nervous system gets a vote in how fast this goes. Healing that respects your pace, not my schedule."  
(Remove the clinical framing, lead with the promise, use active language.)

### **PROBLEM #3: "Culturally Humble" + Long Explanation = Defensive**

**Location:** Homepage Approach section  
**Text:** "Your values aren't something I accommodate—they're something I'm learning from. I don't assume."

**Why it fails:**  
This is trying to say "I'm not a white therapist imposing my values on you," but it's too explanatory. The phrase "I'm learning from you" can sound patronizing (like she's collecting experiences from her clients). "I don't assume" is reactive—it's saying what she *won't do* rather than what she *will do*.

The best copy about values doesn't explain why you have them; it just has them and lives them.

**Better version:**  
"Your community, your family structure, your survival strategies—I'm not here to fix them. I'm here to understand them."  
(Shows action, not explanation.)

### **PROBLEM #6: FAQ Copy Is Too Reassuring, Not Specific**

**Location:** FAQ page  
**Text:** "Is this first-time therapy? Doesn't matter—whether you've done this before or you're walking in cold, I meet you where you are. No shame. No assumptions."

**Why it fails:**  
This is kind but generic. Every therapist meets people where they are. "No shame. No assumptions" is trying to feel supportive but sounds like a disclaimer. It's addressing shame *about* seeking help, not the client's actual shame (which is what they came for).

**Better version:**

### **PROBLEM #12: "Executive Function Difference" Is Clinical, Not Human**

**Location:** ADHD page  
**Text:** "Executive function difference—your brain works differently."

**Why it fails:**  
"Executive function" is the term therapists and coaches use when talking *about* ADHD, not the term someone with ADHD uses to describe themselves. A person thinks: "I can't finish things. I lose my keys. I'm messy." Not "my executive function is different."

**Better version:**  
"Your brain works differently. Time disappears. Starting things is hard. Finishing is harder. And you've spent years thinking you're just lazy."  
(Specific, lived, not clinical.)

## PATTERNS (WHAT'S ACTUALLY WRONG)

### **1. Defensive Rather Than Confident**
The copy often addresses objections before the prospect has them. "No surprises." "No hidden fees." "No judgment." This creates the impression that other therapists *are* surprising, expensive, and judgmental. Better: assume good faith and lead with what you offer, not what you're not.

### **2. Jargon Masquerading as Warmth**
"Trauma-informed," "integrated," "affirming," "culturally humble," "executive function"—these are industry terms that feel cold to someone outside the industry. They signal expertise but alienate the reader. Better to translate them into human language.

### **3. Tone Whiplash**
The About section is vulnerable and specific. The Pricing section is corporate. The Booking section tries to be casual. The FAQ is overly reassuring. The homepage is trying to be everything. A client reading the whole site gets confused about who Rainuka actually is. Better: consistent voice across all channels.

### **4. Vagueness Where Specificity Would Help**
"I meet you where you are." "Real connection, real change." "Therapy that fits the life you actually have." These are all vague. Rainuka's actual value is specific: she understands the intersection of perinatal + ADHD + career transitions. Lead with that. Every generic promise undermines it.

### **5. Corporate Hedging in Personal Space**
Words like "typically," "most," "often," "usually" pepper the site. In an industry where people are making vulnerable decisions, this hedging reads as non-committal. Better: commit to timelines, rates, and what clients can expect. Hedging breeds doubt.

### **6. No Differentiation from Other Therapists**
The values, the copy, the promises—these could apply to hundreds of therapists. What makes Rainuka different? The specific expertise in perinatal + ADHD + career. That's not emphasized nearly enough. Everything else is background noise.
