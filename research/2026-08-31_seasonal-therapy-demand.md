# Seasonal Therapy Demand & Campaign Psychology

## Peak Seasons

### September-October (Back-to-School) — 32% Surge
- **Driver:** Academic transitions, new routines, separation anxiety
- **Who affected:** Students, parents, educators, families
- **Psychology:** Anticipatory anxiety, identity shifts, control anxiety
- **Campaign message:** "Anxiety is normal. Let's manage it together."

### November-February (Holiday + SAD)
- **Driver 1 (Nov-Dec):** Holiday stress, family dynamics, financial pressure, grief
- **Driver 2 (Jan-Feb):** Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD), dark mornings, circadian disruption
- **Who affected:** Families, individuals with SAD, people in grief/transition
- **Psychology:** Isolation, helplessness, compounded grief + seasonal mood
- **Campaign message:** "You don't have to white-knuckle through the holidays."

### January (New Year Reset)
- **Driver:** Goal-setting, fresh starts, resolution energy, accountability
- **Who affected:** Career-focused individuals, people in transition, goal-setters
- **Psychology:** Motivation, but also perfectionism and anxiety about failure
- **Campaign message:** "New year. Real support for real change."

## Campaign Psychology Principles

1. **Timing is everything.** Launch back-to-school campaign by Aug 31 (right now). Launch holiday campaign by Nov 1. Launch new year campaign by Dec 15.
2. **Specificity > generic.** Not "therapy helps" but "Anxiety about school transitions is real, and you can manage it."
3. **Validation + agency.** Acknowledge the struggle, then offer control: "You're not alone. Here's what we can do together."
4. **Micro-CTAs.** Don't just "book now." Offer: "Schedule a 15-min call to see if we're a fit."
5. **Social proof by season.** Feature testimonials from people who faced the same seasonal challenge and succeeded.

## Actionable Insights for Back-to-School Campaign

- **Landing page name:** `back-to-school-anxiety`
