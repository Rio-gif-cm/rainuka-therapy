# Holiday & Winter Season Therapy Demand — Research Summary

**Date:** August 31, 2026  
**Season Window:** November-February (Holiday + Winter Peak)  
**Demand Pattern:** 22-35% increase vs. baseline

---

## Key Research Findings

### 1. Holiday Season Stress (November-December)

**Prevalence:**
- **57%** of Americans report the holiday season as stressful (LifeStance 2025)
- **41%** report MORE holiday stress than previous year (APA 2025, up from 28-29%)
- **32%** report worsening mental/emotional health in Nov-Dec vs. rest of year (Grow Therapy 2025)

**Younger adults hit harder:**
- Gen Z: 57% deprioritize therapy in holiday season
- Millennials: 47% pause sessions
- Age 18-34: 49% expect MORE stress vs. 27% age 65+

**Women disproportionately affected:**
- **37%** of women feel worse mentally in Nov-Dec (vs. 26% of men)
- Gen Xers (45-60, sandwich generation): Highest stress → managing family, kids, aging parents, careers

### 2. Holiday Stressors (What Brings People In)

**Top mental health triggers:**
- **Family dynamics & relationship strain (36%)** — Difficult relatives, old conflicts resurface, performative happiness
- **Weather & reduced daylight (36%)** — Seasonal Affective Disorder onset, circadian disruption
- **Financial pressure (29%)** — Gift-buying, travel, holiday spending strain
- **Loneliness & grief (48%)** — Missing deceased loved ones, isolation despite gatherings
- **Pressure to appear happy (69%)** — Millennial/Gen Z hit hardest (34-35% high pressure)

**Unique Nov-Dec concerns:**
- Grief that intensifies around family gatherings
- Identity shifts (holidays highlight who's missing)
- Work + personal obligations collide (projects due, end-of-year commitments, bonus stress)

### 3. Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD) — October-February Peak

**Prevalence:**
- **~5%** of U.S. adults experience clinical SAD
- **60% female** — Gender-specific risk factors
- **Younger adults (18-30)** show higher susceptibility than older groups
- Peaks: **January-February** (darkest months)

**SAD Symptoms:**
- Low mood, fatigue, hypersomnia (oversleeping)
- Carbohydrate craving
- Loss of interest/motivation (interferes with work, relationships)
- Disrupted circadian rhythm from reduced light

**Psychology:**
- Reduced daylight → melatonin overproduction, serotonin dysregulation
- Circadian rhythm misalignment (body clock desynchronized from clock)
- Worsens alongside holiday stress (Nov-Dec compound effect)

### 4. Seasonal Therapy Demand Patterns

**Therapist Caseload Data:**
- Mental health volume climbs **late fall through winter** (Oct-Feb peak)
- After peak: ease in spring, dip in summer (exception: back-to-school Sep surge)
- **Higher acuity during winter** — More crisis presentations, risk assessments, psychiatric emergencies increase *after* holidays (post-holiday crash)

**Barrier to therapy during holidays:**
- **46%** of therapy clients pause/delay sessions end-of-year (other obligations take priority)
- **42%** of those who paused say lower costs would help them continue
- **37%** say flexible/virtual scheduling would enable attendance

**Opportunity:** Telehealth messaging emphasizing convenience + affordability during holiday crunch.
