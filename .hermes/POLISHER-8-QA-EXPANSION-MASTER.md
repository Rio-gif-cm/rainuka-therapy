## LAUNCH PHASE 2 (MONTH 2): Expanded Coverage (25 Q&As Total: 12 + 13 New)

### LOGISTICS & PRACTICAL QUESTIONS (8-10 NEW Q&As)

8. **"How long is a session, and can I book longer if I need it?"**
   ```
   Standard is 50 minutes. Some therapists do 45, some do 60. I do 50.
   And yes, you can book a 75-minute session if you want—it costs more 
   but sometimes the first session needs that breathing room, or something big 
   comes up mid-way that needs time to land. Just ask.
   ```

9. **"Do I have to come weekly, or can I start with twice a month?"**
   ```
   You get to decide. Some people need weekly—they're in crisis or 
   actively building a new habit. Some start twice a month and shift based 
   on what's happening. Some do intensive (2x a week) for 3 months, then drop to weekly.
   I care more about you having a sustainable rhythm than hitting some 
   'right' frequency. What works for your life?
   ```

10. **"What if I need to cancel a session?"**
    ```
    Life happens. You get one free cancellation per month, as long as you give 
    48 hours notice. After that, cancellations within 24 hours are a charge.
    If something urgent comes up—sick kid, crisis—text me. We'll figure it out.
    The goal is not to punish you for having a life. It's to protect my ability 
    to hold that time for you.
    ```

11. **"What's the actual cost, and do you work with insurance?"**
    ```
    I'm out-of-network with most insurance. That means you pay me directly, 
    and I give you a superbill to submit to insurance if you want.
    Typical reimbursement is 40-60% of what you paid me, depending on your plan.
    I charge $[X] per 50-minute session. For actual numbers, I'll send you an intake 
    form that covers all the cost scenarios—single sessions, ongoing, multiple weeks.
    ```

12. **"When can I actually book? Do you have evening/weekend availability?"**
    ```
    I work Monday-Thursday, 10am-6pm, and occasional Friday mornings. 
    Most of my open slots are afternoons (2-6pm). I don't do weekends.
    If that doesn't work for your schedule, I can recommend therapists 
    who do evening hours. I'd rather point you somewhere that fits than 
    squeeze you into a time that stresses you out.
    ```

13. **"What do I bring to my first session?"**
    ```
    Just yourself. Maybe your insurance card if you want to check on coverage.
    I'll send you intake forms the day before—basic history, medications, 
    what brings you in, insurance info. You can fill it out digitally or I 
    can do it with you in session. Either way.
    ```

14. **"How do I actually book? Is there a phone number or do I fill out a form?"**
    ```
    [To be filled in with actual booking process]
    Online calendar: [Link]
    Or email: [Email]
    Either way, you'll get a confirmation and the intake form link.
    ```

15. **"Can you work with me if I have no insurance and limited budget?"**
    ```
    Yes. I have a sliding scale for people with genuine financial hardship.
    That means we talk about what you can actually afford and build a plan around that.
    Some clients do weekly at the base rate. Some do twice a month. 
    What matters is sustainability, not maximizing my billable hours.
    ```

### RELATIONSHIP BOUNDARIES (5-6 NEW Q&As)

16. **"What if I get a crush on you, or start to have feelings?"**
    ```
    That's more common than you think, and it's not weird. There's a closeness 
    in therapy that can feel like intimacy. It's a different kind.
    
    If it happens, we talk about it. Not to shame you—to understand it.
    Usually it's about safety or being seen. And that's useful information.
    
    Boundary: We won't become friends after therapy. That's not about me not 
    liking you. It's about protecting the work. Therapist-client relationships 
    are different from friendships, and that difference matters.
    ```

17. **"Will you remember me if I take a break? Or what if I run into you in public?"**
    ```
    I remember my clients. Not as vague blurs. As actual people with actual stories.
    If you take a 6-month break and come back, I'll remember where you left off.
    
    If you run into me at a grocery store? I won't approach you unless you approach me.
    That's your privacy. And if you want to say hi, we can—I'll keep it brief, 
    knowing you might not want people in your life to know you're in therapy. 
    That's yours to decide.
    ```

18. **"Can I text you between sessions?"**
    ```
    Email is better than text. Not for therapy—for safety planning or check-ins.
    'I'm having the intrusive thoughts we talked about' or 'Something shifted and 
    I want to remember to bring it up Thursday'—yes.
    
    Crisis texting? That goes to my emergency line, and I'll call you back.
    But ongoing therapy-between-sessions doesn't work via text. It's too easy 
    for it to replace the session, and you deserve 50 minutes of full presence, 
    not fragmented digital therapy.
    ```

19. **"What's your policy if I'm suicidal or having crisis thoughts?"**
    ```
    We have a protocol. I'll give it to you on our first session.
    Crisis number, my emergency contact, what you do if it's 3am and you're terrified.
    
    You're not a burden for having a crisis. You're someone I can help navigate it.
    And if at any point you need psychiatric hospitalization, I'll help with that transition.
    ```

20. **"What if we don't click? Can I switch therapists?"**
    ```
    Yes. No hard feelings. Sometimes it's not about competence—it's chemistry.
    If three months in you're thinking 'this isn't the right fit,' tell me.
    I'll help you transition to someone else. And I'll give them notes so you 
    don't have to re-tell your whole story.
    ```

21. **"Are there things I shouldn't talk about in therapy?"**
    ```
    You can talk about anything. The only boundary is confidentiality limits:
    If you tell me you're going to harm yourself or someone else, or if there's 
    child abuse, I'm mandated to report. I'll tell you that's happening.
    
    Beyond those legal limits? Everything's fair game. Rage, shame, lust, anger at me—all of it.
    ```

## LAUNCH PHASE 3 (MONTH 3-4): Deep Shame Categories (15+ New Q&As)

### ABOUT THE THERAPY ITSELF (Process & Expectations) — 5-6 Q&As

22. **"What's your actual approach? What modality do you use?"**
    ```
    I primarily work somatically—meaning I pay attention to your body, 
    not just your words. What does anxiety feel like in your chest? 
    Where do you hold your grief?
    
    I also use some Internal Family Systems (IFS) when it's useful—the idea 
    that you have different parts (your protector, your critic, your creative self) 
    and sometimes they're in conflict.
    
    And sometimes we just talk. Because sometimes that's what's needed.
    
    I don't have one protocol I push everyone through. I work with what you need.
    ```

23. **"How long will I be in therapy? Weeks? Years?"**
    ```
    Depends on what you're working with. Someone processing a breakup might 
    need 3-6 months. Someone unraveling a lifetime pattern might need 2+ years.
    
    The honest version: You're not 'done' when you don't feel bad anymore. 
    You're done when you understand the pattern well enough to navigate it yourself.
    
    I'll tell you: I don't have an incentive to keep you forever. 
    My job is to help you become more capable, not more dependent.
    ```

24. **"How do I know if therapy is actually working?"**
    ```
    You'll know. Because over time:
    - You notice things about yourself you didn't before
    - You handle situations differently (less reactivity, more choice)
    - Your relationships shift (clearer boundaries, less resentment)
    - You feel less alone with your own mind
    
    You won't feel amazing all the time. But you'll feel more resourced. 
    More like yourself. Less like you're white-knuckling through life.
    ```

25. **"Will you try to convince me to do something I don't want to do? (Leave my partner, quit my job, etc.)"**
    ```
    No. My job isn't to direct your life. It's to help you see what's actually happening 
    so you can make a choice that's truly yours.
    
    If you're in a relationship that's damaging you, I'll say so. But I won't say 'leave.'
    That's your decision. I can help you think through the decision, but the choice is yours.
    ```

26. **"What if you think I need medication? Can you prescribe?"**
    ```
    No, I don't prescribe. But I can recommend you see a psychiatrist or your doctor 
    if I think medication might help.
    
    Therapy + medication works for some people. Therapy alone works for others. 
    Both are legitimate. My job is to help you figure out what you need.
    ```

### SHAME-SPECIFIC CONTENT (Perinatal, ADHD, Burnout, Intimacy, Suicidality) — 8-10 Q&As

27. **"I'm struggling with postpartum rage. Not sadness—rage. Is that normal?"**
    ```
    Postpartum can absolutely be rage. And some people just rage. 
    Especially if you've been holding it your whole life.
    
    Rage isn't bad. It's usually telling you something matters, or a boundary 
    got crossed. So instead of medicating it away, we find out what it's saying.
    And then you get to choose: express it, transform it, or set a boundary 
    so it doesn't happen again.
    
    Society tells women rage is 'too much.' Rage is just information.
    ```

28. **"I have intrusive thoughts. Am I going to act on them? Am I dangerous?"**
    ```
    Intrusive thoughts ≠ suicidal ideation ≠ dangerous impulse.
    
    Intrusive thoughts are: 'What if I jumped off this bridge?' Followed by 
    'Holy shit, I don't want to die, where did that come from?'
    
    Suicidal ideation is: 'I can't take this. I want to die.' It's different.
    
    Both are things we can work with. Both are more common than you think. 
    And neither means you're broken or dangerous. It means your nervous system 
    is in overdrive. We can regulate that.
    ```

29. **"I'm really struggling with money. I feel ashamed. Does that come up in therapy?"**
    ```
    Constantly. Money is deeply connected to safety, control, and shame.
    And yes, it's hard to pay for therapy when money is the problem.
    
    We can talk about that directly. Sometimes that's the first thing we address—
    how money affects your nervous system. And if therapy feels financially 
    unsustainable, we can look at different schedules or find you a sliding-scale option.
    ```

30. **"I'm struggling with my ADHD. Will you judge me for messiness, procrastination, or time blindness?"**
    ```
    No. I have ADHD. I get it from inside.
    
    That thing where you meant to do the thing but suddenly it's 11pm and you didn't?
    Or you hyperfocus and forget to eat? Or you know you should start your taxes 
    but the task feels impossible even though it's 'simple'?
    
    Those aren't character flaws. That's ADHD. And we can work with it.
    ```

31. **"I'm afraid of being 'too much' in relationships. I take up too much space."**
    ```
    That usually means someone told you—explicitly or implicitly—that your needs 
    were inconvenient. That happened, and it stung.
    
    But here's what's true: Your needs aren't too much. You might just be with 
    someone who doesn't have capacity for them. That's a compatibility issue, not a you issue.
    
    In therapy, you get to take up space. All of it. And we'll figure out 
    what healthy reciprocal relationships actually look like.
    ```

32. **"I'm struggling with sex/intimacy after trauma. Will you understand?"**
    ```
    Yes. Sex after trauma is complex. You might have no desire. Or you might 
    use it compulsively to feel in control. Or you might freeze during intimacy.
    All of those make sense. None are character flaws.
    
    I've worked with a lot of trauma survivors in the bedroom. It's not something 
    I'm uncomfortable with. And I know—without you explaining—that this is 
    usually about feeling safe in your own body, not about your partner.
    ```

33. **"I feel like I'm failing at motherhood/parenthood. Is that something you work with?"**
    ```
    Constantly. And the first thing I want to say is: 'Feeling like you're failing' 
    usually means you care a lot.
    
    Perfect parenting doesn't exist. But responsive parenting—where you notice 
    you messed up and repair it—does. That's what we build toward.
    
    And if you're dealing with postpartum anxiety/depression on top of it? 
    We address that first, because it changes everything.
    ```

34. **"I burned out spectacularly and I'm terrified of making the same choice again."**
    ```
    That fear is smart. Because burnout usually means something about your system needs to change.
    
    Sometimes that's the job. Sometimes it's the way you work. Sometimes it's both.
    
    We figure out: What led to this burnout? What did your body try to tell you 
    that you didn't hear? And then: What would look different this time?
    
    You don't have to white-knuckle your way to another crash.
    ```

### BOUNDARIES & MY LIMITATIONS (3-4 Q&As)

35. **"Are there things you won't work with? Or clients you'd turn away?"**
    ```
    Yes. I'm not the right therapist if:
    - You're primarily seeking someone to validate staying in an abusive situation
    - You're looking for medication management (I don't prescribe)
    - You're in active addiction without commitment to sobriety
    - You're primarily suicidal and need psychiatric hospitalization
    
    All of those are legitimate needs—they just need different support than I offer.
    I'd rather refer you to the right person than pretend I'm equipped for something I'm not.
    ```

36. **"What's your stance on [controversial topic: politics, religion, lifestyle choice]?"**
    ```
    My stance doesn't matter. Your life does.
    
    I might have personal beliefs, but my job isn't to impose them. 
    It's to help you figure out what's true for you.
    
    If I ever find myself judging your choices instead of understanding them, 
    that's worth talking about. Because it means I'm not the right therapist for you.
    ```

37. **"If I tell you about illegal activity, will you turn me in?"**
    ```
    Depends on what it is. Confidentiality has limits.
    
    I'm mandated to report if there's child abuse, elder abuse, or if you're 
    planning to harm someone. Beyond that, I don't care about your legal status.
    
    But we should be clear about this from the start so you're not scared to tell me things.
    ```

### FINAL SAFETY CHECK / INTAKE Q&As (2-3 Q&As)

38. **"What happens if I can't afford ongoing therapy? Can I just do a few sessions?"**
    ```
    Yes. Some people do one session. Some do six. Some do ongoing.
    
    The honest truth: Some work happens in longer-term therapy, but real shifts 
    can happen in short-term work too. We work with what's possible for you.
    
    And if money changes—you get more capacity later—you know where to find me.
    ```

39. **"How private is this? Will anyone know I'm seeing a therapist?"**
    ```
    I keep confidentiality seriously. Your information isn't shared unless:
    - You give me written permission
    - There's a legal mandate (abuse, harm to self/others)
    - Your insurance asks questions (which is rare)
    
    Confidentiality is between us. You don't have to tell anyone you're in therapy.
    ```

40. **"I want to try therapy but I'm terrified. Where do I start?"**
    ```
    You start by saying that: 'I'm terrified.'
    
    That's literally where everyone starts. 
    
    You can book a 20-minute free consultation. We talk about what's going on, 
    whether working together makes sense, and what first session would look like.
    
    No pressure. No commitment. Just a conversation to see if this is right for you.
    
    And if it's not? That's information too. I'll help you find someone it might be right with.
    ```
