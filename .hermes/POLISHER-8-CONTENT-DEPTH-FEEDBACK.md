## 📋 CONCEPT 1: "What Therapy Actually Feels Like" — DEPTH AUDIT

### Current State: ✅ Strong Structure, ⚠️ Incomplete Arc

**What's Working:**
- ✅ Clear 5-stage walkthrough (First 10 min → Story → Listening → Reframe → End)
- ✅ Addresses core client anxiety ("Will I cry? Will she judge me?")
- ✅ Shows Rainuka's actual method (body listening, not interpretation)
- ✅ Warm, specific language ("nervous system is the truth-teller")

**Critical Gaps:**

#### GAP #1: Missing "The First 5 Minutes" (The Real Arrival Experience)
**Current:** Jumps to "waiting room" vaguely  
**Problem:** Clients arrive with terror. No detail on:
- What the physical space feels like (is there a couch? do you sit facing?)
- How Rainuka greets (handshake? how close?)
- First words (friendly small talk or straight to business?)
- Permission to be nervous (does she normalize it?)

**Proposal:**
```
[NEW SECTION: THE ARRIVAL (First 5 Minutes)]

"Before we sit down, before any of this starts, there's the arrival.

You walk in. Maybe your hands are shaking. Maybe you're trying to figure 
out if you should sit on the couch or the chair across from it.

Here's what I do: I meet you where you are. Not 'sit here' (clinical). 
More like: 'Come in. Can I get you water? No pressure either way.' 

There's a moment where you're both figuring out how to be together. 
I might ask something small—'How was the drive?' or 'First time doing this?'—
not because I need to know about traffic. Because I want you to land here.

You might say 'I'm nervous.' I'll say 'Yeah. Most people are. That's not 
a sign something's wrong. It means you care about this.'

And then we sit. You're not on a couch with me across a desk (the blank screen thing). 
We're angled toward each other, close enough to hear a whisper, far enough 
that you're not in each other's space. You can choose how visible you are.

This part matters. This is where you're deciding: 'Can I trust this person?'
I know that. I'm not rushing it."
```

#### GAP #2: Missing "What Makes You Comfortable" (Autonomy & Boundaries)
**Current:** No mention of client agency in session structure  
**Problem:** Anxious clients want to know:
- Can I ask to pause?
- Can I say "I'm not ready for that question"?
- Can I change my mind mid-session?
- Is there a signal to slow down or speed up?

**Proposal:**
```
[ADD TO SECTION 3: THE LISTENING]

"There's something I want you to know about how I work: You're not 
on a table with me deciding what needs to happen. You have agency here.

If I ask something and you freeze, you can say 'I'm not ready for that.'
I'll ask differently or drop it entirely. Your comfort is not optional.

If you need me to slow down—if I'm going too deep, too fast—you can say that.
If you need to speed up, to stop talking and just sit, that works too.

There's no 'right way' to be vulnerable in this room. 
There's just your way."
```

#### GAP #3: Missing "What Happens After" (Post-Session Reality)
**Current:** Ends abruptly at session conclusion  
**Problem:** Clients don't know:
- Will I be emotional afterward?
- Can I text between sessions?
- What if I panic Monday night?
- Do you remember what we talked about?

**Proposal:**
```
[NEW SECTION: AFTER THE SESSION]

"You walk out. You might feel light. You might feel stirred up.
You might feel nothing yet—the weight of what you just said taking a day to land.

All of those are normal.

Here's what happens on my end: I take notes after you leave. 
Not during—I'm present with you. But after, I write down what 
we touched, what's important to follow. I'll remember.

Between sessions, you can email me. Not for therapy—I don't do therapy over email.
But if something urgent comes up? 'I'm having intrusive thoughts' or 'I need 
to know if what I said was okay'? I'll respond within 24 hours with clarity.

Crisis stuff (self-harm, suicide, you're in danger)? I have a protocol for that.
I'll give you that on our first session—a crisis number, my emergency contact, 
what you do if it's 3am and you're terrified.

And here's the thing about ongoing therapy: Most people feel worse before 
they feel better. That's not a sign to stop. That's a sign the work is working.
But I'll know the difference between 'this is therapeutic stirring' and 
'this approach isn't right for you.' We'll check in."
```

#### GAP #4: Missing "What I Won't Do" (Setting Expectations for Boundaries)
**Current:** Implies positive boundaries but doesn't name restrictions  
**Problem:** Clients need to know:
- Will you give me advice?
- Will you tell me what to do about my relationship?
- Do you have an opinion on my career change?
- Will you fix me?

**Proposal:**
```
[ADD TO SECTION 4: THE REFRAME]

"There's a difference between what I do and what I don't do.

I won't tell you what to do with your relationship. ('Leave him' or 'Stay.')
I won't decide your career for you. I won't fix you—you're not broken.

What I will do: Help you see what's actually happening. Ask questions 
that make you curious about your own behavior. Show you patterns you didn't see.
Then you decide. Because it's your life, not my theory.

People sometimes want me to just tell them the answer. 'Should I leave?'
The honest version: I don't know. But I know how to help you know.
And that's the more useful thing."
```

## 💭 CONCEPT 3: "Things Clients Actually Ask Me" (Q&A) — DEPTH AUDIT

### Current State: ✅ Good Starter Set, ⚠️ Only 30% of Real Questions

**What's Working:**
- ✅ Real questions (childhood, crying, cost, medication, vulnerability, worse after therapy)
- ✅ Warm answers that validate
- ✅ Rainuka's actual voice ("I have tissues. I don't get uncomfortable")
- ✅ Normalizes hard feelings

**Critical Gaps:**

#### GAP #1: Missing "Logistics" Category (40% of Client Anxiety)
**Current:** Partially covered (cost, medication, vulnerability)  
**Problem:** Clients Google these at midnight:
- How long is a session? (50 min? Can I have longer?)
- How often do I need to come? (Weekly? Can I start twice a month?)
- What if I need to cancel? (Cancellation fee? How much notice?)
- Do you take my insurance? (Out of pocket cost?)
- What's your availability? (Evenings? Weekends?)
- How do I actually book? (Phone? Email? Online form?)
- What do I bring to first session? (Forms? Insurance card? Just myself?)

**Proposal (New Q&A Set: "Getting Started"):**
```
---

Q: "How long is a session, and can I book longer if I need it?"

"Standard is 50 minutes. Some therapists do 45, some do 60. I do 50.
And yes, you can book a 75-minute session if you want—it costs more 
but sometimes the first session needs that breathing room, or something big 
comes up mid-way that needs time to land. Just ask."

---

Q: "Do I have to come weekly, or can I start with twice a month?"

"You get to decide. Some people need weekly—they're in crisis or 
actively building a new habit. Some start twice a month and shift based 
on what's happening. Some do intensive (2x a week) for 3 months, then drop to weekly.
I care more about you having a sustainable rhythm than hitting some 
'right' frequency. What works for your life?"

---

Q: "What if I need to cancel a session?"

"Life happens. You get one free cancellation per month, as long as you give 
48 hours notice. After that, cancellations within 24 hours are a charge.
If something urgent comes up—sick kid, crisis—text me. We'll figure it out.
The goal is not to punish you for having a life. It's to protect my ability 
to hold that time for you."

---

Q: "What's the actual cost, and do you work with insurance?"

"I'm out-of-network with most insurance. That means you pay me directly, 
and I give you a superbill to submit to insurance if you want.
Typical reimbursement is 40-60% of what you paid me, depending on your plan.
I charge $[X] per 50-minute session. For actual numbers, I'll send you an intake 
form that covers all the cost scenarios—single sessions, ongoing, multiple weeks."

---

Q: "When can I actually book? Do you have evening/weekend availability?"

"I work Monday-Thursday, 10am-6pm, and occasional Friday mornings. 
Most of my open slots are afternoons (2-6pm). I don't do weekends.
If that doesn't work for your schedule, I can recommend therapists 
who do evening hours. I'd rather point you somewhere that fits than 
squeeze you into a time that stresses you out."

---

Q: "What do I bring to my first session?"

"Just yourself. Maybe your insurance card if you want to check on coverage.
I'll send you intake forms the day before—basic history, medications, 
what brings you in, insurance info. You can fill it out digitally or I 
can do it with you in session. Either way."
```

#### GAP #2: Missing "Relationship Boundaries" Category (Shame Prevention)
**Current:** Touches vulnerability but doesn't address relational fears  
**Problem:** Clients wonder:
- Can we be friends after therapy?
- Will you judge my relationship choices?
- What if I have feelings for you?
- Can I text you between sessions?
- Will you remember me if I take a break?
- What if I run into you at Whole Foods?

**Proposal (New Q&A Set: "About Our Relationship"):**
```
---

Q: "What if I get a crush on you, or start to have feelings?"

"That's more common than you think, and it's not weird. There's a closeness 
in therapy that can feel like intimacy. It's a different kind.

If it happens, we talk about it. Not to shame you—to understand it.
Usually it's about safety or being seen. And that's useful information.

Boundary: We won't become friends after therapy. That's not about me not 
liking you. It's about protecting the work. Therapist-client relationships 
are different from friendships, and that difference matters."

---

Q: "Will you judge my relationship? Like, if I'm in a bad situation?"

"I won't judge. But I also won't lie to you.
If you're describing something that concerns me—abuse, betrayal, 
a pattern that's hurting you—I'll say so. Not as judgment. As care.

My job isn't to validate every choice you make. It's to help you see 
clearly what's actually happening, and then you decide."

---

Q: "Will you remember me if I take a break? Or what if I run into you in public?"

"I remember my clients. Not as vague blurs. As actual people with actual stories.
If you take a 6-month break and come back, I'll remember where you left off.

If you run into me at a grocery store? I won't approach you unless you approach me.
That's your privacy. And if you want to say hi, we can—I'll keep it brief, 
knowing you might not want people in your life to know you're in therapy. 
That's yours to decide."

---

Q: "Can I text you between sessions?"

"Email is better than text. Not for therapy—for safety planning or check-ins.
'I'm having the intrusive thoughts we talked about' or 'Something shifted and 
I want to remember to bring it up Thursday'—yes.

Crisis texting? That goes to my emergency line, and I'll call you back.
But ongoing therapy-between-sessions doesn't work via text. It's too easy 
for it to replace the session, and you deserve 50 minutes of full presence, 
not fragmented digital therapy."
```

#### GAP #3: Missing "About Therapy Itself" Category (Expectations)
**Current:** Some coverage (worse after therapy, crying, vulnerability)  
**Problem:** Clients don't understand:
- What modalities do you actually use? (Somatic? IFS? EMDR? Psychodynamic?)
- Will this take forever? (How long am I in therapy?)
- How do I know it's working?
- Can therapy make me feel worse long-term?
- What if we don't click?

**Proposal (New Q&A Set: "About the Work"):**
```
---

Q: "What's your actual approach? What modality do you use?"

"I primarily work somatically—meaning I pay attention to your body, 
not just your words. What does anxiety feel like in your chest? 
Where do you hold your grief?

I also use some Internal Family Systems (IFS) when it's useful—the idea 
that you have different parts (your protector, your critic, your creative self) 
and sometimes they're in conflict.

And sometimes we just talk. Because sometimes that's what's needed.

I don't have one protocol I push everyone through. I work with what you need."

---

Q: "How long will I be in therapy? Weeks? Years?"

"Depends on what you're working with. Someone processing a breakup might 
need 3-6 months. Someone unraveling a lifetime pattern might need 2+ years.

The honest version: You're not 'done' when you don't feel bad anymore. 
You're done when you understand the pattern well enough to navigate it yourself.

I'll tell you: I don't have an incentive to keep you forever. 
My job is to help you become more capable, not more dependent."

---

Q: "How do I know if therapy is actually working?"

"You'll know. Because over time:
- You notice things about yourself you didn't before
- You handle situations differently (less reactivity, more choice)
- Your relationships shift (clearer boundaries, less resentment)
- You feel less alone with your own mind

You won't feel amazing all the time. But you'll feel more resourced. 
More like yourself. Less like you're white-knuckling through life."

---

Q: "What if we don't click? Can I switch therapists?"

"Yes. No hard feelings. Sometimes it's not about competence—it's chemistry.
If three months in you're thinking 'this isn't the right fit,' tell me.
I'll help you transition to someone else. And I'll give them notes so you 
don't have to re-tell your whole story."
```

#### GAP #4: Missing "Shame Specifics" Category (Validation for Secret Stuff)
**Current:** Touches on a few shame topics (childhood, crying, worse after therapy)  
**Problem:** Clients won't ask these directly, so they stay hidden:
- Sex and intimacy after trauma
- ADHD shame (messiness, time blindness, failure feels)
- Postpartum rage (not sadness—rage)
- Suicidal thoughts (not plans, just ideation)
- Financial stress and shame
- Being "too much" in relationships
- Attraction to things that "shouldn't" interest you

**Proposal (New Q&A Set: "The Shame Stuff"):**
```
---

Q: "I'm struggling with sex after trauma. Will you judge me?"

"No. Sex after trauma is complex. You might have no desire. Or you might 
use it compulsively to feel in control. Or you might freeze during intimacy.
All of those make sense. None are character flaws.

I've worked with a lot of trauma survivors in the bedroom. It's not something 
I'm uncomfortable with. And I know—without you explaining—that this is 
usually about feeling safe in your own body, not about your partner."

---

Q: "I have intrusive thoughts about harming myself. Does that mean I'm suicidal?"

"Intrusive thoughts ≠ suicidal ideation. Intrusive thoughts are: 'What if 
I jumped off this bridge?' Followed by 'Holy shit, I don't want to die, 
where did that come from?'

Suicidal ideation is: 'I can't take this. I want to die.' It's different.

Both are things we can work with. Both are more common than you think. 
And neither means you're broken or dangerous. It means your nervous system 
is in overdrive. We can regulate that."

---

Q: "I rage. Not cry, not sadness—rage. Is that postpartum?"

"Postpartum can absolutely be rage. And some people just rage. 
Especially if you've been holding it your whole life.

Rage isn't bad. It's usually telling you something matters, or a boundary 
got crossed. So instead of medicating it away, we find out what it's saying.
And then you get to choose: express it, transform it, or set a boundary 
so it doesn't happen again.

Society tells women rage is 'too much.' Rage is just information."

---

Q: "I'm really struggling with money. I feel ashamed. Does that come up in therapy?"

"Constantly. Money is deeply connected to safety, control, and shame.
And yes, it's hard to pay for therapy when money is the problem.

We can talk about that directly. Sometimes that's the first thing we address—
how money affects your nervous system. And if therapy feels financially 
unsustainable, we can look at different schedules or find you a sliding-scale option."
```

## 💡 KEY INSIGHT: "The Unasked Question"

The biggest gap across all 3 concepts: **What doesn't Rainuka do / what are the real limitations?**

Clients are asking:
- "Will you tell me what to do?"
- "Will you fix me?"
- "Can I text you anytime?"
- "Do you have to report me if I say X?"
- "Can you work with me if I'm suicidal?"

Current content implies warmth + availability but doesn't explicitly answer what the boundaries are. This creates anxiety for clients wondering if they're "too much."

**Recommendation:** Add a section to Concept 3 Q&A: **"About My Limitations"**
```
Q: "Are there things you won't work with? Or clients you'd turn away?"

"Yes. I'm not the right therapist if:
- You're primarily seeking someone to validate staying in an abusive situation
- You're looking for medication management (I don't prescribe)
- You're in active addiction without commitment to sobriety
- You're primarily suicidal and need psychiatric hospitalization

All of those are legitimate needs—they just need different support than I offer.
I'd rather refer you to the right person than pretend I'm equipped for something I'm not."
```

This actually *increases* trust by being honest about limitations.
