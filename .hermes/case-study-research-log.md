- **Testimonial Psychology Research** (`research/2026-08-31_social-proof-testimonial-psychology.md`):
  - Key finding: **Specificity > Vagueness** in creating credibility
  - Narrative structure: Hook (pain) → Transformation (change) → Forward movement
  - Trust signals: Expertise + Trustworthiness + Contextual Understanding
  - Outcome metrics matter: Functional improvement (return to work, relationships, clarity) beyond just symptom reduction

### Decision: Case Studies vs. Testimonials
**Why case studies matter:**
- Testimonials are 1-3 quotes (powerful but brief)
- Case studies are full narratives (500-800 words per story)
- Cases allow for context-building: before/after clarity, Rainuka's specific approach, realistic timelines
- Cases demonstrate expertise through specificity (RSD in ADHD + couples? Perinatal partner dynamics?)

## Key Insights from Brainstorm

1. **Case studies fill different role than testimonials:** Testimonials = quick social proof (quotes). Cases = narrative depth + expertise demonstration.

2. **Niche specificity builds trust:** Generic therapy stories don't differentiate. "RSD in ADHD couples" or "postpartum partner perspective" positions Rainuka as expert.

3. **Outcome authenticity matters:** "Solved the problem" = unbelievable. "Moved from crisis mode to functional capacity" = credible.

4. **Narrative arc > Clinical explanation:** "I was terrified about intrusive thoughts" is more memorable than "She had postpartum intrusive thoughts OCD."

5. **Warmth + expertise isn't a tradeoff:** Can be both. Rainuka's phrases like "Your brain isn't broken—you're in broken conditions" combine clinical accuracy with human warmth.
