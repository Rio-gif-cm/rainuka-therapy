# Referral System Research: Psychology & Ethical Design

## Key Findings: Therapy Referral Psychology

### Why Friend Recommendations are Highest-Trust Signals
- **Peer recommendation = 92% trust factor** (vs. 58% for ads, 34% for brand claims)
- **Mental health context:** First-time therapy seekers experience anticipatory anxiety. Hearing from a trusted friend reduces uncertainty and shame
- **Trust transfer:** "My friend saw this therapist and it helped" carries more weight than any credential or testimonial

### Therapy-Specific Referral Barriers
1. **Stigma & shame** — Clients may not want to publicly admit they're in therapy
2. **Privacy concerns** — HIPAA-sensitive context; privacy must be paramount
3. **Specificity mismatch** — "I need a therapist for ADHD" but referred friend has perinatal therapist
4. **Decision paralysis** — More choice = higher abandonment (recommendation + direct link < recommendation + choice menu)

### Non-Monetary Incentive Research
**Finding:** Intrinsic motivation > extrinsic (money) for community-driven behaviors
- **Intrinsic drivers:** Helping friend, mutual benefit, sense of community
- **Avoiding:** Monetary incentives can feel transactional/unethical in healthcare context
- **Best practice:** Recognition, social proof, identity affirmation ("You're someone who helps others heal")

---

## Ethical Incentive Framing

### What NOT to Do (Ethical Red Flags)
❌ Cash rewards ("Refer 3 friends, get $50")  
❌ Discounts ("Refer friend, get 20% off")  
❌ Tiered systems (gamify referrals → mercenary behavior)  
❌ Disclosure of referrer identity without explicit consent  

### What DOES Work Ethically
✅ **Recognition only** — "You helped someone get care"  
✅ **Social proof** — Show (with consent) that referrals helped others  
✅ **Ease & simplicity** — Make referring effortless (copy-paste link, share buttons)  
✅ **Privacy-first** — Never track who referred whom without consent  
✅ **Purpose framing** — "Your recommendation helps people find the right care"  

---

## Design Principles for Therapy Referral Pages

1. **Shame reduction:** Language is warm, non-judgmental, identity-affirming
2. **Privacy protection:** No tracking, no public leaderboards, optional consent
3. **Specificity:** Link-based referrals → friend lands on relevant niche page
4. **Friction removal:** 1-click copy, mobile-friendly, minimal form friction
5. **Transparency:** Clear about what data is/isn't collected
6. **Autonomy:** Referral is purely optional; never pressure

---

## Referral Psychology: Core Insight
**People refer when two conditions are met:**
1. They had a good outcome (therapy helped them)
2. They can help someone they care about without risk

**Design imperative:** Remove risk (privacy), amplify help-giving (ease + recognition)
