# DEI in Therapy Practice Marketing: Research & Best Practices

## Key Insights from Therapy-Specific Research

### Why Diversity Statements Matter
- **Trust Signal:** BIPOC, LGBTQ+, neurodivergent, disabled, and low-income clients actively look for explicit affirming language before booking
- **Risk Reduction:** Absence of diversity messaging is read as a red flag ("they haven't thought about this") rather than humility
- **Conversion Impact:** Therapists with explicit diversity commitments report higher booking rates from marginalized communities

**Source:** Josephine Hughes (therapy marketing research); DrVy Therapy; Everwell Health case studies

---

## Core Components of Authentic DEI Statements (Therapy-Specific)

### 1. **Lived Experience + Humility**
- Name your own identity and positionality (where you hold power, privilege, bias)
- Avoid: "I work with all populations equally"
- Prefer: "As a [identity], I recognize my blind spots and actively work to address them"
- **Why:** Clients want evidence you've done self-work, not just generic acceptance

### 2. **Affirmation Over Acceptance**
- "I work affirmatively with LGBTQ+ clients" > "I work with LGBTQ+ clients"
- "I affirm your neurodivergence" > "I accept ADHD/autism"
- "We work WITH your brain, not against it" (neurodiversity model)
- **Why:** Marginalized communities have been pathologized; affirmation signals repair

### 3. **Avoid Performative Language**
- ❌ Generic checklists of identities
- ❌ Corporate DEI jargon ("diverse talent," "underrepresented groups")
- ❌ Conditional acceptance ("I work with LGBTQ+ clients if they're open to...")
- ✅ Specific, actionable, grounded in actual practice

### 4. **Name Specific Marginalized Communities**
- Instead of vague "diverse backgrounds," name: BIPOC, LGBTQ+, neurodivergent, disabled, trans, non-binary, low-income, immigrant, first-generation
- **Why:** Specific naming signals you've thought about each community's unique needs, not just generic diversity

### 5. **Commitment to Ongoing Learning**
- "I don't claim to have all answers"
- "I continue learning, reflecting, staying accountable"
- "I welcome feedback and course-correction"
- **Why:** Therapists who acknowledge limits build more trust than those claiming expertise in all identities

## Therapy-Specific DEI Focus Areas

### For Perinatal Clients
- Intersectional pregnancy/parenting (race, sexuality, family structure, disability, income)
- Systemic racism in maternal health (higher mortality/morbidity for BIPOC mothers)
- Reproductive justice framework (not just mental health, but bodily autonomy + systemic access)
- LGBTQ+ family building (non-traditional paths, legal barriers, grief around loss)

### For ADHD Clients
- Racialized misdiagnosis patterns (boys of color underdiagnosed; white women over-diagnosed)
- Shame + identity work for late-diagnosis adults (especially BIPOC)
- Masking in contexts of survival (neurodivergence + racism/homophobia/classism)
- Access barriers (BIPOC communities often have limited ADHD specialists)

### For Career Clients
- Career decisions shaped by structural inequality (racism, sexism, classism, ableism)
- Burnout as response to systemic oppression, not personal failing
- "Choosing" careers within systemic constraints
- Income anxiety for low-income first-generation clients

### For All Clients
- **Disability affirming:** Therapy accessible for disabled clients (sensory needs, mobility, cognitive load, cost)
- **Economic access:** Transparent pricing, sliding scale options, no surprise fees
- **LGBTQ+ safety:** No assumptions about sexuality/gender, affirmation of all family structures

## Best-Practice Examples (Analyzed)

### DrVy Therapy Model (Strongest)
✅ Names lived experience: "Asian American woman, daughter of non-English speaking immigrants, first-generation college student from low-income background"
✅ Specific commitments: "I stand firmly against racism, sexism, homophobia, transphobia, ableism"
✅ Learned humility: "I don't claim to have all answers... cultural competence is ongoing practice"
✅ Systemic awareness: "How culture, systems, and power shape mental health"
**Pitfall avoided:** Doesn't create false claims of expertise in all identities

### Everwell Health Model (Community-Centered)
✅ "No tolerance for intolerance, bigotry, oppression, injustice"
✅ "Many of our therapists are members of the communities we serve"
✅ "Brave space" framing (not just safe—actively challenging)
✅ Dedicated to "underrepresented, underserved, historically marginalized populations"
**Strength:** Emphasizes practice culture, not just individual therapist

### Josephine Hughes Model (Therapist-Focused)
✅ Clear welcome statement (customizable per therapist)
✅ "Commitment to ongoing learning in supervision"
✅ Framework for naming positionality without overexplaining
✅ Permission to "get it wrong" + learn from feedback
**Strength:** Accessible, gives therapists language without perfectionism

## Antiracism + LGBTQ+ Affinity in Therapy Marketing

### Language to Include
- **For BIPOC:** "I understand systemic racism shapes mental health" OR "Therapy for clients navigating racialized stress, discrimination, identity"
- **For LGBTQ+:** "I work affirmatively with LGBTQ+ clients, including trans and non-binary folks" (specificity matters)
- **For Disabled:** "Disability-affirming therapy; I'm committed to access and accommodation"
- **For Low-Income:** "Sliding scale available; no barriers to accessing care based on income"

### Language to Avoid
- "I treat everyone the same" (erases systemic inequality)
- "Colorblind approach" (ignores racialized experience)
- "I work with the LGBTQ+ community if they're open to..." (conditional acceptance = red flag)
- "Diversity is our strength" (corporate; performative without specifics)
