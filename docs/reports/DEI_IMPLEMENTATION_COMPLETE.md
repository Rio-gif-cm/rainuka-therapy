## Content Structure

### "My Commitment to Accessibility & Inclusion" Section

#### Communities Explicitly Affirmed
1. **BIPOC clients** — racialized stress, discrimination, identity, systemic inequity
2. **LGBTQ+ clients** — identity celebration, affirming care (trans/non-binary specificity)
3. **Neurodivergent clients** — ADHD/autism affirmation, strengths-based model
4. **Disabled clients** — accessibility, accommodation, anti-cure-narrative
5. **Low-income/first-generation clients** — cost accessibility, sliding scale availability

#### Actionable Practice Commitments (6 core)
- ✓ Ongoing personal bias/privilege work (not one-time)
- ✓ Humility + learner stance (welcome feedback/course-correction)
- ✓ Systemic awareness (oppression shapes mental health, context matters)
- ✓ Cultural centering (your values > my clinical assumptions)
- ✓ Genuine accessibility (transparent pricing, sliding scale, no surprise fees)
- ✓ Accountability (clinical consultation, ongoing training, direct feedback)

#### Closing Statement
"This commitment isn't performative or something I 'also do.' It's baked into how I show up every session. If you ever feel like your identity, background, or experience isn't being honored, I want to know. Feedback helps me be better."

## Research Key Findings

### Why This Matters
- **Trust Signal:** BIPOC/LGBTQ+/disabled clients actively look for explicit affirmation before booking
- **Red Flag Absence:** Silence on DEI is read as "they haven't thought about this," not humility
- **Conversion Impact:** Explicit diversity commitments correlate with higher inquiry rates from marginalized communities

### Authenticity Framework
✅ **DO:**
- Name specific communities (not vague "diverse backgrounds")
- Use affirmation language ("work affirmatively with LGBTQ+ clients")
- Acknowledge ongoing learning/limitations
- Ground in actual training/practice
- Address systemic issues (racism, ableism, homophobia shape mental health)

❌ **AVOID:**
- Generic checklists of identities
- Corporate DEI jargon
- Conditional acceptance ("if they're open to...")
- Performative language
- Over-claiming expertise in all communities
