## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Audit of rainuka-therapy.com found **4 core therapy clichées** embedded in pages:

## FINDINGS: WHERE THE CLICHÉES LIVE

### 🔴 CLICHÉE #1: "Trauma-Informed"

**Current Usage:**
- `app/about/page.tsx:` "I work trauma-informed, culturally humble, practical."
- `app/about/page.tsx:` "Through trauma-informed assessment..."
- `app/perinatal/page.tsx:` "Compassionate, trauma-informed support"
- `app/couples/page.tsx:` "A skilled therapist trained in trauma-informed couples work"
- `app/lgbtq/page.tsx:` "I provide affirmative, trauma-informed care grounded in queer psychology"
- `app/privacy/page.tsx:` "This privacy policy is part of our trauma-informed design commitment"

**The Problem:**
- Every therapist claims this now; it's become meaningless
- Doesn't explain *how* Rainuka actually works with trauma
- Feels like compliance language, not a promise

**Why It Stays Generic:**
- Doesn't answer: "What do you actually *do* with trauma? How is it different?"

## APPLIED EXAMPLES: WHERE TO USE THESE

### Example 1: ABOUT PAGE Bio Section
**Current (Line 88-91):**
> "I work trauma-informed, culturally humble, practical. Therapy isn't about fixing you—it's about understanding yourself better so you can build a life that actually fits. I show up as a real person, not a blank screen. You're not getting clinical distance; you're getting someone who has sat in the hard places and knows the work it takes to move through them."

**PROPOSED REPHRASE:**
> "I notice when your nervous system is protecting you—hypervigilance, avoidance patterns, the ways you've learned to survive. I know how to help you understand *why* those patterns made sense. Then, if they're holding you back now, I teach you practical ways to dial them down. I'm trained in how trauma lives in your body, not just your story. And I'm honest: you're not broken. You were surviving intelligently with what you knew."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Specific (nervous system, patterns, body-based)
- ✅ Not clinical (conversational, concrete)
- ✅ Differentiated (most therapists say "trauma-informed"; Rainuka *explains* what that means)
- ✅ Aligns with her actual work (evident in case studies, FAQ)

---

### Example 2: PERINATAL PAGE Hero
**Current (Line ~40):**
> "Compassionate, trauma-informed support for pregnancy loss, postpartum anxiety, infertility grief."

**PROPOSED REPHRASE:**
> "Someone who knows that grief and relief can show up at the same time. That anxiety after a baby isn't weakness—it's your nervous system on overdrive. That infertility shame lives in isolation. Here, your feelings get named, not pathologized. We understand the invisible part: how you're carrying all of this while everyone else sees you as fine."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Honors complexity (grief AND relief, anxiety as symptom not flaw)
- ✅ Validates the hidden experience (invisible, shame, gap between outside/inside)
- ✅ Not clinical (no "trauma-informed" placeholder)
- ✅ Specific to perinatal (names pregnancy loss, infertility, postpartum specifically)

---

### Example 3: BACK-TO-SCHOOL PAGE Child Transitions
**Current (Line ~120):**
> "Therapy gives your child a safe space to practice handling transitions."

**PROPOSED REPHRASE:**
> "Your child learns to name what they're feeling instead of shutting down. They practice speaking up when they're confused or scared. They see that big emotions don't break the room or the relationship. And you get tools to recognize what they need before they melt down."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Specific outcomes (naming, speaking up, tolerating emotion)
- ✅ Includes parent in the benefit (not just "safe space for child")
- ✅ Concrete, observable (not vague "safe" promise)
- ✅ Aligns with how therapy actually works

---

### Example 4: LGBTQ PAGE Opening
**Current (Line ~30):**
> "I provide affirmative, trauma-informed care grounded in queer psychology."

**PROPOSED REPHRASE:**
> "I don't ask you to minimize your queerness or navigate safety on your own. Discrimination isn't in your head—it's in your world. We work *knowing* that, rooted in queer psychological research on resilience, identity, and chosen family. What you're experiencing isn't a disorder; it's a reasonable response to an unreasonable context."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Acknowledges systemic reality (discrimination is real, not pathology)
- ✅ Specific to queer experience (chosen family, identity navigation)
- ✅ Grounded in psychology without jargon
- ✅ More powerful than "affirmative care" checkbox

---

### Example 5: DISABILITY-ACCESS PAGE
**Current (Line ~95):**
> "It's a real part of your life that deserves compassionate, accessible support."

**PROPOSED REPHRASE:**
> "Your disability isn't the thing we're fixing in therapy. Your disability is part of how your body and mind work. Therapy is about building a life that actually fits your disabled self—access that works, relationships that aren't exhausting, and understanding which struggles are yours and which are the world failing to accommodate you."

**Why This Works:**
- ✅ Moves past "compassionate" to actual disability justice framing
- ✅ Specific (access, relationships, understanding systemic failure)
- ✅ Empowering (not about fixing the disability)
- ✅ Differentiates from ableist therapy language
